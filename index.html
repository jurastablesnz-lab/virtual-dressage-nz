The problem

Amateur riders juggle a lot — on their own.

Between coach visits, most riders train alone. Plans live in notebooks, tests are learned from paper diagrams, judge sheets get stuffed in the truck and videos sit unwatched on a phone.

Scattered information

Show dates, entries, test sheets, coach homework and results live in different places, making it hard to see progress.

Limited coaching time

Coach visits are precious and infrequent. Without a plan in between, lessons fade before the next session.

Feedback goes unused

Judge comments and competition video hold the best clues for improvement — but turning them into schooling work takes time and expertise.

Our story

From lockdown arenas to a rider’s app.

Virtual Dressage NZ didn’t start as an app. It started as a way to keep New Zealand riders riding, together, when shows stopped.

Chapter I

Born in lockdown

When Covid lockdowns stopped competitions across New Zealand, riders had nowhere to ride a test. Virtual Dressage NZ ran virtual dressage competitions: riders filmed their tests at home and they were judged online.

Chapter II

Riders return

When lockdowns lifted and riders went back to shows, the idea grew. Virtual Dressage NZ became a way to capture, share and track progress between competitions.

Chapter III

The app

That evolved into a training, planning and tracking app, now in beta and tested on Samson.

Harmony is trained, one stride at a time.

Every Grand Prix test began with a single walk-trot schooling session.

Features

Everything for the season, in one place.

Today — your daily dashboard

Open the app and know exactly what to do.

  • Key stats: best score, next show, entry deadlines
  • Built-in freestyle music player
  • Weekly plan checklist and today’s session
  • Super 5 league points at a glance

Season — plan the campaign

See the whole road to the championships.

  • Super 5 show countdown to Nationals
  • Training phases: Foundation, Consolidate, Strength, Championship
  • Know which phase you’re in and what it’s for

Tests — learn every movement

An interactive arena diagram steps through each test.

  • Movement-by-movement arena path
  • What the judge is looking for
  • Coefficients flagged so you know where marks count
  • Schooling tips for each movement

Coach — make every lesson count

Plan coach visits so nothing is wasted.

  • Warm-up and exercises
  • What to film
  • Homework to carry into the week

Log — results & logistics

Track your record and never forget the hoof oil.

  • Results history and averages
  • League points tally
  • Show packing checklist

Video review

Upload competition videos for your coach to review remotely — feedback between visits, wherever you both are.

Judge-sheet insights

Photograph your judge sheets to get a read of the test and three schooling options to work on next — turning comments into a plan.

Chapter III, in the saddle

Built by a rider. Tested on Samson.

Virtual Dressage NZ was created by Susan Philp, a rider from the Palmerston North area, to train her own horse Samson — a 14-year-old black stationbred by Tiraumea Flint, competing at Level 2 Novice.

It’s the latest chapter of the Virtual Dressage NZ story, which began with virtual competitions during lockdown. Every screen was shaped by real schooling sessions, real coach visits and real show days. It solves the problems Susan hit herself: keeping a season plan on track, learning tests properly, and getting the most from limited coaching.

Samson’s best Level 2 score so far, tracked in the app: 66.2%.

The levels we all dream of — Virtual Dressage NZ helps riders build toward them, from Novice up.

Business model

How Virtual Dressage NZ could make money

For funders & partners

An innovation opportunity, built in New Zealand.

Market

Dressage is practised by amateur riders, coaches and clubs across New Zealand and internationally, and equestrian sport is broadly an engaged, high-spend community. (General market observation — sizing to be researched.)

Innovation

Applying AI to the rider’s own evidence: reading photographed judge sheets and pairing them with competition video to produce specific, actionable schooling options — feedback between coach visits.

NZ-first

Designed around the New Zealand competition calendar, tests and league structure, with the goal of growing from a local pilot into an exportable sport-tech product.

A track record of innovation and community

Virtual Dressage NZ has already adapted twice: from online competitions in lockdown to a progress-sharing platform when shows returned. The app is the next step, grounded in a real rider community.

What funding would accelerate

R&D on AI judge-sheet and video analysis, a structured pilot with NZ riders and clubs, accessibility and data-privacy work, and preparation for launch.
